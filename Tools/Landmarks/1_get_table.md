Orion Nebula (M42) / Trapezium Cluster	H II region / young cluster	-330	-182.9	-132.9	The nearest large star-forming region and stellar nursery visible to the naked eye, hosting the young Trapezium Cluster.	At roughly 400 pc, the Orion Nebula is the closest large-scale massive star formation event to the Sun, making it the best-studied stellar nursery in the sky. The nebula's glow comes from gas ionized by the hot young stars of the Trapezium Cluster at its core, and the surrounding Orion A cloud continues to form stars actively today. Because it sits deep in the dust map (roughly 130 pc below the Galactic plane relative to the Sun, and ~380 pc toward the outer Galaxy), it's a key calibration target for any 3D dust reconstruction.
